> *(Alexander Belyaev, *Under the Arctic Sky*, 1939)*

**Silent Hint: Townfail** (or, as Russian distributors prefer to call it, *The Omen: Resident Error*) is a veritable hymn to Soviet science, technology, and collective labor, wrapped by the boys from **KOMONEY** in the shell of a classic first-person shooter!

Back in the distant year of 1939, Alexander Romanovich could never have imagined that, almost a hundred years later, his hero Jim Jolly would become an action hero in the style of that very nuclear blonde guy in sunglasses, whose three-dimensional adventures on Ken Silverman's engine turned the world of today's oldfags upside down exactly thirty years ago!

It's funny that the boom-bap playing in the game, with its distinctly jazzy '90s flavor, was inspired by a motif from the undeservedly forgotten 1980s Soviet blockbuster *I Have the Honor*, starring Alexander Kahun - a man who could have wiped the floor with Schwarzenegger, who appeared alongside Kevin Peter Hall in what was then the 'hot new thing.'

None of this should really come as a surprise, though, when you remember that the game itself was inspired by a remarkably obscure book by a now largely forgotten writer, published eighty-seven (sic!) years ago.

Far more interesting is this: given all the madness and chaos unfolding in the story, why didn't KOMONEY add episodes featuring stealth at a railway station (including stealing a train), a cruise on a steamboat with Superman-style leaps straight from the deck onto a bridge, and even a terrorist operation to blow up a prisoner barge?

All this magnificence, filmed by Moldovan filmmakers during the years of perestroika and set to utterly drugged-out music by a composer from a little town near Oryol, would seem to fit perfectly into this game, which is trying to imitate *Duke Nukem 3D* and the first *Heretic* from 1994.

And remember the lines of Velimir Khlebnikov heard in that film:

> "The slums and the forest were filled with a sound,  
> Groaning and ringing all over the ground.  
> So that, oh, so that - with a spear in his hand,  
> The hunter might finish the beast of the land!"

What more could one want as an extra spit in the face of the *Predator* (which they originally wanted to call 'Hunter', no less)?

> "Say, Pelkin, would you leave your North behind,  
> To seek the sunny Florida you'd find?"
>
> "Of course not!"
>
> "There you have it, friend:  
> True paradise begins where Earth's cold poles extend!"

No, say what you will, frost is a wonderful thing. As Pushkin put it?..

> "...mighty winter, fierce and bold,  
> Leads her armies, icy-cold.  
> Frost and snow in shaggy flight,  
> March against us day and night..."

Or, as Nekrasov has it:

> "Frost the Voivode makes his rounds,  
> Where frozen silence shrouds the grounds."

What can one say - a voivode. Not someone to fool around with. But if you know how to take hold of him, even he can be turned from a voivode into our humble servant. The same principle applies to stern samurai and their Scottish slaves: a couple of deft moves, and before they know it, their fearsome game has turned into a farce.

For what could be easier than taking their colorful (and slightly singed) comrade, handing him a Soviet shovel, and making him use this weapon of the proletariat to clear away the consequences of what the builders of communism have wrought beyond the Arctic Circle?

Yes, yes, our chocolate friend, now known as Jim Jolly (that is, 'cheerful James,' as opposed to the gloomy widower James Sutherland from Bloober's Poland), by the will of fate finds himself in the Arctic. More precisely, not far from Cape Chelyuskin, where the so-called 'Ice City' was once being built with great enthusiasm. The enlightened minds of communism decided to turn the underground caves beneath the construction site into a genuine resort: they hung an artificial sun from the ceiling, planted all sorts of tropical vegetation, and released an entire menagerie down there.

However, the laws of the genre demand that such a scam should end up going completely to hell. And so their sun goes out, the plants wither, and the horses breathe... the foul breath of death! And our comrade Jim, slightly roasted by the artificial sun, is left to clean out these very Augean stables.

He will be assisted by an elven artifact - a crossbow, declared a weapon of heretics. After all, it does not fire ordinary arrows, but magical projectiles - three at a time, just like in that famous tale from *Raven Software* (or was it *Falco* after all?).

Along the way, our chocolate friend will encounter maddened chickens (because socialism needs eggs!), genetically modified pumpkins (remember: there is no Halloween in the USSR!), walking dea... er, I mean, corn cobs (Khrushchev is turning over in his grave as we speak), and animated garden scarecrows - stern in the Soviet manner, rather than glamorous like the ones those Chinese showed us in the story of the anime fox-girl Hinako.

> "Chief, I think you should study the Guardian Birds more closely."
>
> *(Robert Sheckley, *The Guardian Bird*, 1953)*

And don't forget the vehicles that have risen up against their masters - the very ones made for manure and mud, not for transporting millionaire asses - as well as the great and terrible Guardian Birds, spawned from the imagination of Robert Sheckley, a brilliant science-fiction writer whose work the capitalist world has unjustly left in the shadows.

True, here they have grown feathers, and after being hit by the elven crossbow they amusingly hang suspended right there in mid-air, but they still glare maliciously at Jim, as if reminding him: "Metal and electrons reason logically, but not as people do."

But the main hit of the season, without a doubt, was the Kolobok - the very same one who, according to an old joke, hanged himself. No one, however, expected that afterward he would also turn into a zombie (or, after the ascension of Eve Lyne to the throne of the gods, would it be more politically correct to call him a 'molded'?).

From the bites of this infection-ridden, hug-obsessed companion, our unjolly Jim will have to restore his health with red apples that grow straight out of the ground like roses. And no, of course, these are definitely not the exact same fruits that the aforementioned fifty-three-inch goddess wore in her hair like trendy accessories.

> "Once upon a time, by Granny's side,  
> A little gray goat lived and hopped with pride,  
> Oh, little gray goat, so small and sweet,  
> Oh, little gray goat with nimble feet!"
>
> *(Sergey Snegov, *Humans as Gods, Part Two: Invasion into Perseus*, 1968)*

And of course, how could we do without the little gray goats wandering here and there - the absolute favorites of André Scherstyuk? Some particularly impressionable individuals consider these adorable beasts to be almost the embodiment of Satan in general and the Antichrist in particular. There may be some truth to that - at least if we consider that these horned comrades intend to impale our cheerful Jim on their horns. And they're not the only ones: the rest of the evil residents of this amazing 'resistive evocation' intend to do exactly the same.

In short, as you have probably realized by now, nobody is going to give Jim any breaks - for the original program of this amazing menagerie, together with its plants, gardening implements, and assorted household equipment, contained one simple rule: if all other means of fighting for the bright future should fail, the capitalist world is to be simply destroyed - starting, naturally, with something small: our Jim Jolly.

So all that remains for our ebony protagonist is to put his faith in the spirits of *Corvus* and *Duke Nukem* and carry out a total cleansing of all this Soviet filth. And accompanying this noble cause in the name of capitalism will be a well-worn record of New York hip-hop, in which, however, one can distinctly hear the unmistakable flavor of Oryol Oblast - or, more precisely, the glorious city of Bolkhov.
